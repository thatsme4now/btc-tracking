₿ Bitcoin Portfolio — Visualizations

Beyond the dashboard's table view, the app offers dedicated visualizations for exploring your portfolio's history. This page is a living reference — it currently covers the Flow Diagram and the Holdings View, and will grow as more chart types are added (a “Coming soon” slot already exists in the app itself).

VISUALIZATION 01

Flow Diagram (Sankey)

Open it from the dashboard header via “Visualization” → “Flow Diagram” (or directly at /btc-tracking/flow). It draws a Sankey diagram of how your BTC moved over time: purchases flow in from the left, sales and external withdrawals flow out to the right, and transfers between your own wallets/exchanges are shown as connections between position nodes. Bookings are bundled per month and per source/target pair so the diagram stays readable even with a long history.

Filters

FilterEffect
From / ToRestrict the diagram to a date range
PositionAnchor the diagram on one wallet/exchange — traces its BTC upstream (where it came from) and downstream (where it went)

Color legend

  • Buy — BTC purchased into a position
  • Sell — BTC sold out of a position
  • Transfer — moved between two of your own positions
  • External — deposit from, or withdrawal to, an address outside your tracked positions

Transaction panel

Next to the diagram is a transaction panel listing every underlying booking as a card (date, position, type, BTC amount, price, fees, currency, exchange rate, transfer ID, comment). Buy, sell and transfer in/out each get a small colored badge. A search box above the list filters by any value shown on the cards (position, type, date, amounts, comment, …) — regardless of whether you type amounts with a dot or a comma. On narrower screens the panel moves below the diagram instead of sitting beside it.

Interacting with the diagram

ActionResult
Hover a link or nodeTooltip with the exact amount, month, position balance and the list of individual bookings behind it
Click a node or linkHighlights it and filters the transaction panel down to only the related bookings; the panel title switches to “Filtered: …” with a button to show all again. Clicking the same element again clears the filter; clicking a different one switches immediately.

Interacting with the transaction panel

ActionResult
Hover a transaction cardTemporary preview highlight of the matching link/nodes in the diagram (only while no diagram selection is active)
Click a transaction cardPins the highlight for that exact booking in the diagram — without filtering or collapsing the list, and without changing the panel title/count. Click the same card again to unpin; click a different card to switch the highlight immediately.
ℹ Why two different click behaviors? Clicking directly in the diagram is meant for drilling down (“show me only what belongs to this node/link”). Clicking a card in the already-visible list is meant for quick visual lookup (“where in the diagram is this one booking?”) without losing your place in the full list.
VISUALIZATION 02

Holdings View

Open it from the dashboard header via “Visualization” → “Holdings View” (or directly at /btc-tracking/holdings). It breaks your portfolio down year by year across three charts: what you bought and sold, what that meant in realized and unrealized gain/loss, and how your total BTC balance evolved.

Purchases & sales chart

One bar pair per year: purchases, stacked by wallet/exchange (each position gets its own shade of blue/turquoise, so you see both the yearly total and where it went through), next to a separate sales bar (purple) for the same year.

Realized & unrealized gain/loss chart

A second chart, one bar group per year, with two series: realized gain/loss (sale proceeds minus the weighted-average cost basis of the BTC sold — i.e. an actual profit/loss figure, not just the raw sale amount) and unrealized gain/loss (portfolio value at that year's end minus cost basis at that point). Both are green when positive, red/orange when negative. Unrealized gain/loss for the current, still-running year uses the live BTC price; past years use the 31.12. reference price described below — if a past year has none stored yet, its bar is simply missing until one is filled in.

BTC balance chart

A third chart shows the total BTC balance across your whole portfolio as of 31.12. of each year (now, for the current year), in Bitcoin orange.

ℹ Reference prices for past years: the app keeps an approximate BTC year-end (31.12.) price per currency for past years, seeded once in the background — this is what powers the unrealized gain/loss bars for past years above. A table below the charts lets you view and correct these values yourself, and fill in a year the background seed doesn't cover yet (e.g. a year that only just ended).
